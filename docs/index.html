Offline RFID attendance for classrooms

Attendance in a tap. Records in your own folder.

TapIn takes class attendance from RFID cards or by hand, and keeps every class as ordinary Excel and JSON files in a folder you choose. No server. No account. No internet needed.

What it does

Built for one person running a real classroom

Not a platform to sign up for. A desktop app you install, point at a folder, and use for the term.

Tap to record

Students tap a card and the reader's screen shows their name back. TapIn marks each one Present, or Late once the session is past your Late after limit.

No reader? Still works

Manual entry, Mark all present and Mark absent… work with no hardware at all. The reader only makes a large class faster; nothing is hidden without it.

Your files, not our database

Each class is a .json file for its settings and an .xlsx file for its roster and attendance. Back up by copying the folder; open the spreadsheets in Excel whenever you like.

See who needs attention

The dashboard counts students at risk, and each class names them: those who have missed enough sessions that they are close to failing your attendance policy.

Statistics that answer questions

The Present / Late / Absent split, the trend against your policy, a weekday-and-time heatmap, a comparison of every class, and a printable report.

Session history

A calendar of every day you recorded a session across your active classes. Pick a day to see which classes met and open them.

Bring your lists in

Upload your university's student list report, or a simple sheet headed Student Number and Name Surname (.xlsx, .xls, .ods, .csv). Not sure how it should look? Download a sample. Import whole classes from a spreadsheet too.

Export anything

The roster and the attendance grid as Excel, a student's history and your class list as CSV, a certificate of attendance and the statistics report as PDF, and any chart as an image.

Hard to lose a record

If TapIn closes before you submit, your scans are saved as a draft and offered back. If a workbook is open in Excel, records queue and retry. A zip backup is made when a changed folder opens; the newest five are kept.

Quick to move around

Search classes and students from the top bar (Ctrl F), open the command palette with Ctrl K, jump between pages with Ctrl 1–4, start a class with Ctrl N. On a Mac, use ⌘.

English and Türkçe

The whole interface switches between English and Turkish at once, dates and weekday names included.

Light, dark, or your system's

Pick a theme, make everything 90 % to 130 % bigger or smaller, switch glass to solid panels, or show classes on one line each. This page has the same theme switch, top right.

How it works

From a folder to a finished class in five steps

The same path the in-app tour walks you through.

  1. 1

    Choose where your data lives

    The first time TapIn opens it asks where to keep your classes, rosters and attendance. Pick any folder, or use ~/TapIn. From then on TapIn opens that folder by itself and does not ask again.

    • You only see this screen again if the saved folder can't be opened, and it says why: for example that it is open on another computer.
    • Switch to another folder at any time in Settings → Data. Nothing in the folder you leave is moved or deleted.
  2. 2

    Add a class

    A four-step editor: Details (code, name, term, attendance policy, late limit, weeks and hours), Schedule (the days it meets and the times), Students, and Colour & review.

    • Add students by hand, upload your university's student list report, or copy them from another class. The upload screen can save a sample file with the headings a simple list needs.
    • Duplicate a class for next term, pin the ones you use most, archive the ones you don't.
  3. 3

    Take attendance

    Open the class and press Take attendance. With a reader, students tap their card and are marked as they go. Without one, mark them by hand on the same screen. Nothing is saved until you press Submit.

    Present Late Absent
    • An unknown card? Register it to a student on the spot, and they are recorded straight away. A card belongs to one student, across every class.
    • Undo the last record, mark everyone present, or mark absentees in bulk.
    • Close TapIn by accident and your unsubmitted scans are saved as a draft, offered back the next time.
  4. 4

    See where you stand

    Each class names its students at risk under your attendance policy. Statistics shows the split, the trend with your policy line, a weekday-and-time heatmap and a comparison of all your classes. History shows every session, a month at a time.

    • Open a student to see every session and their card, or double-click a cell in the grid to correct a record.
    • Download a printable PDF report for any class.
  5. 5

    Take it with you

    Export the roster or the attendance grid as Excel, a student's history or your class list as CSV, a certificate of attendance or the statistics report as PDF, and any chart as a PNG or SVG picture.

    • Because every class is already an ordinary file, there is nothing to export in order to leave: copy the folder.
The reader

An optional card reader

TapIn works with an ESP32-based RFID reader over a USB cable, over the reader's own Wi-Fi hotspot, or over Bluetooth. It is a convenience, not a requirement: every feature works by hand.

ESP32 + RC522Reads the card and sends its serial number
USB, Wi-Fi or BluetoothCable by default; the board can host its own hotspot, or pair over Bluetooth
TapInLooks up the student and records the session
Your data folderPlain files on your own disk

The board only ever sends a number

The reader has no roster and no idea who anyone is. It sends a card's serial number; TapIn looks up who that is and sends the name back for the reader's screen. Names never live on the device, so a reader that is lost or taken apart gives up nothing about your students.

board → app  04A3B2C1
app → board  OK:20260001|Ayşe Çelik
app → board  INVALID

On the reader, a green light means the card was recorded; a red light and a beep mean it was refused. Its 16×2 screen shows the student's number and name (without accents: the screen has no Turkish letters) or a short message such as Invalid card.

  • USB cableNothing to set up. Plug the reader in and TapIn finds the port when you open Take attendance.
  • Its own Wi-FiThe reader hosts a small network your computer joins. It does not touch your institution's network, which also means no internet on this computer while scanning.
  • Bluetooth NewPair the reader with your computer, then pick its serial port in Settings. It needs the Bluetooth version of the reader's firmware. Tried with a real reader on Linux; Windows and macOS have not been tried yet.

Wi-Fi mode, in five steps

Settings → Card reader walks you through it, and has a Test connection button that tells you whether this computer is on the reader's network, whether the reader answers, or that it was found.

Want to build a reader of your own? See Build the reader.

Build the reader

Parts, wiring and firmware

The reader is an ESP32 board with an RC522 card module, a 16×2 screen, four lights and a buzzer. Here are the three sketches, one for each way of connecting, and a circuit you can run in your browser before you buy anything.

The three sketches

The wiring is the same for all three, so one board can run any of them. They are built with the Arduino-ESP32 core 3.3.12 and the MFRC522 and LiquidCrystal_I2C libraries, and released under the MIT licence.

  • rc522_serial_reader.inoUSB cable
  • rc522_wifi_reader.inoIts own Wi-Fi network, TapIn-Reader
  • rc522_bluetooth_reader.inoBluetooth Classic. New, and tried on Linux only

The parts list, the pin-by-pin wiring tables, the lights and what they mean, flashing steps and a troubleshooting table are in the user guide.

Try it without a board

Wokwi is a free simulator that runs in your browser. It can run the USB sketch against a pretend ESP32, RC522 reader, screen and lights, and lets you pretend a card was tapped.

  1. Start a projectOpen a new ESP32 project on wokwi.com
  2. Replace its three filesdiagram.json, sketch.ino and libraries.txt
  3. Press PlayThe screen says “Ready to scan”. The Wi-Fi and Bluetooth sketches are not meant for the simulator
Settings

Make it yours

One page, five sections: General, Data, Card reader, Updates, and Help & about.

  • Language

    English or Türkçe. Changes every screen at once, with no restart.

  • Theme

    Dark, Light, or System, which follows your computer's own setting and switches while TapIn is open.

  • Interface size

    90 %, 100 %, 115 % or 130 %: text, buttons and lists grow or shrink together.

  • Reduce transparency & compact lists

    Solid panels instead of glass, if that reads better, and one line per class in Classes.

  • Close after inactivity

    Closes TapIn when nobody has used it for a while, so a shared computer isn't left open. It never closes while you have unsubmitted scans.

  • Data folder & backups

    Show the folder, copy its path, or switch to another one. When a changed folder opens, TapIn saves a zip backup in .backups and keeps the newest five.

  • Settings file

    Export your language, interface size, class list and inactivity choices to a file, and import it on another computer.

  • Updates, off by default

    Turn it on and TapIn asks GitHub at startup whether a newer version exists. Nothing is downloaded or installed.

  • Help & about

    The tour, the FAQ and the privacy notes are one click away, any time.

Privacy

Where the data goes

The same notes the app shows under Settings → Help & about → Privacy.

By design

Nothing leaves your computer

TapIn has no accounts, no cloud service and no analytics. It sends no usage data and no crash reports. Out of the box the only network connection it makes is to your own card reader, on the reader's own local network.

One exception, and it is off

The update check

Settings has a switch called Check for new versions. It is off. If you turn it on, TapIn asks github.com once at startup for the version number of the newest release, and tells you in Notifications if yours is older. The request carries no student data, no data folder path and no identifier. Nothing is downloaded or installed. Leave the switch off and TapIn never contacts github.com.

What is stored

Numbers, names, attendance, card IDs

Each student's number and name, their attendance records and, if you use the reader, the ID of the card they registered. TapIn asks for nothing else: no email addresses, no photographs, no grades. It all lives in the data folder you chose, as ordinary .json and .xlsx files.

Worth being plain about

The files are not encrypted

Anyone who can open the data folder can read every student's name, number and attendance, because the spreadsheets open directly in Excel by design. The data is private because the folder is. Keep it somewhere only you can reach, treat a backup zip the same way, and remember that the law and your institution's rules (KVKK in Turkey, GDPR in the European Union) apply to how you keep it. This is not legal advice: if you are unsure, ask your university's data protection office.

Removing TapIn never deletes your records: your data folder stays exactly where you put it.

Installing

First run, step by step

TapIn 3 opens data folders made by TapIn 2, so an upgrade keeps your classes. After that, the user guide covers every screen.

Windows 10 / 11

  1. Run the setup fileTapIn-x.y.z-setup.exe, 64-bit
  2. Click through the wizardIt installs for your user: no administrator password needed unless you choose to install for everyone
  3. Start it from the Start menuRemove it from Settings → Apps like any other program

Windows SmartScreen may warn on first run because the installer is not code-signed. Choose More info → Run anyway.

macOS 13+ (Apple Silicon)

  1. Unzip the downloadIt contains TapIn.app
  2. Move it to ApplicationsThen open it
  3. Allow it onceThe app is not signed by Apple, so macOS blocks the first launch: open System Settings → Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway

This build is for Macs with an Apple chip (M1 or newer, arm64) running macOS 13 or newer. There is no Intel build yet.

Ubuntu 24.04+

  1. Install the packageDouble-click it, or use the command below
  2. Find TapIn in your applications menuOr run tapin in a terminal
  3. Remove it any timesudo apt remove tapin
sudo apt install ./tapin_*_amd64.deb

64-bit only. Built and tried on Ubuntu 24.04 and later, where the package also installs a small security profile so TapIn's sandbox can start.

Check your download (SHA-256)

Each download above shows a checksum: a fingerprint of the file. After you download, work out the checksum of your copy and compare. The two must be identical; if not, download again.

Windows (PowerShell)  Get-FileHash .\TapIn-*-setup.exe -Algorithm SHA256
macOS                 shasum -a 256 TapIn-*-macos.zip
Linux                 sha256sum tapin_*_amd64.deb

Every release also has a SHA256SUMS.txt file with all of them, on its release page.

FAQ

Common questions

Do I need the RFID reader to use TapIn?

No. Every class can be taken with manual entry, which is a fully supported way to work. The reader only makes a large class faster; nothing is hidden or disabled without hardware.

Where is my data kept?

In the data folder you chose when TapIn first started, and nowhere else. Each class is one .json file for its settings and one .xlsx file for its roster and attendance. You can open those .xlsx files in Excel whenever you like.

How do I back up my data?

Copy the data folder. That is the whole backup: there is no database or hidden state anywhere else. TapIn also takes its own zip snapshot into the folder's .backups folder each time it opens and something has changed, keeping the five most recent.

Can I move my data folder to another computer?

Yes. Copy the folder across and point TapIn at it from Settings → Data. There is no licence key, no activation and no machine binding. Do not, however, keep one data folder in a synced cloud folder and open it on two computers at once.

Why does TapIn say my data folder is open on another computer?

The data folder is in a synced folder (OneDrive, Dropbox, iCloud) and another computer still has it open. Two computers saving to one folder would overwrite each other's attendance with no warning, so TapIn refuses. Close TapIn on the other computer first. If that computer is off or crashed, delete the .lock file in the data folder.

A student's card was refused. Why?

Usually the card belongs to someone else. TapIn names the real holder when it refuses, which normally turns out to be a student handing over a friend's card. A card can only ever belong to one student, across every class.

A student is in two of my classes. Do they register their card twice?

No. Registering a card once binds it to that student everywhere they appear, so it works in every class they are enrolled in. If they join a new class later, their existing card is recognised the first time they tap it.

What happens if a student loses their card?

Open the class, open the student, and choose Remove card. The card stops being recognised, and a new one can be registered the next time the student taps.

What happens if TapIn cannot save attendance?

It queues the records inside the data folder and retries automatically the next time it opens, so nothing is lost if the workbook was open in Excel or the disk was briefly unavailable. The Dashboard tells you when a queued batch has gone through.

Can I edit the Excel files directly?

You can open and read them any time. Avoid editing them while TapIn is running, because your changes could be overwritten the next time it saves, and a file left open in Excel will block TapIn from saving, which it will tell you about.

Does the Bluetooth reader work on my computer?

Bluetooth is new in TapIn 3. It has been tried with a real reader on Linux, where it paired, reconnected every time and recorded real card taps. It has not been tried yet on Windows or macOS, so use the USB cable or the reader's own Wi-Fi if you need to be sure. The steps for your system are in Settings → Card reader → Bluetooth.

Does the Wi-Fi reader need internet?

No, and it does not connect to your institution's network either. The reader hosts its own small network that your computer joins directly. That does mean the computer has no internet while scanning.

Is there a password or login?

No. TapIn has no accounts and trusts whoever is using the computer, because your operating system already decided that. If the machine is shared, protect the data folder the way you would protect any other folder of student records, and consider the Close after inactivity setting.

What should my student list file look like?

TapIn reads three kinds of sheet (.xlsx, .xls, .ods or .csv), and finds the headings by their labels, not by counting rows. One is your university's student list report, exactly as the university gives it. The other two are simple: Student Number and Name Surname in the first two columns, or Student Number, Name and Surname in the first three. Write the headings exactly like that. A ready-made file is one click away: student-list-sample.xlsx or student-list-sample.csv. TapIn can also save the same sample from the upload screen. For importing whole classes, see class-list-sample.xlsx or class-list-sample.csv.

Can I take attendance on a Saturday or Sunday?

Yes. A class's schedule can have any of the seven days, from Monday to Sunday, and a class can meet more than once on the same day.

Do I have to restart TapIn after changing the language?

No. Switch between English and Türkçe in Settings and every screen, date and weekday changes at once.

Which versions of Windows, macOS and Ubuntu do I need?

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. macOS 13 or newer on a Mac with an Apple chip (M1 or newer). Ubuntu 24.04 or newer, 64-bit. The downloads above say the same.

Does TapIn follow KVKK and GDPR?

TapIn keeps student data on your own computer and sends it nowhere, which makes it easy to keep. But the law applies to the person who keeps the records, and that is you: KVKK in Turkey and GDPR in the European Union both treat attendance records as personal data. Keep the data folder where only you can reach it, tell your students what you record, and follow your university's rules. This is not legal advice; your university's data protection office can tell you what applies.

What does it cost?

Nothing. TapIn is free to download and use: no subscription, no account and no licence key.
